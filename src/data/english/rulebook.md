# ZeroLore Rulebook

ZeroLore is designed to be flexible, modular, and adaptable. If this is your first time playing, start by getting comfortable with the basics of units before jumping into faction abilities, weapons, and unit specialties, adding these concepts as you become more familiar with the game.

**This rulebook provides a solid foundation, but players are free to create scenarios, campaigns, and additional rules while maintaining the fundamental structure of the system.**

---

# Core Concepts

Before you start playing, it is important to clarify some basic concepts used throughout the rulebook. ZeroLore uses simple, direct language, but these definitions avoid confusion during the game.

### Miniatures, Units, and Squads

A **miniature** represents an individual combatant, creature, or vehicle on the battlefield.

A **unit** is the basic activation element of the game.

- In **Skirmish mode**, each miniature acts as an independent unit.
- In **Grand Battle mode**, a unit represents an organised group of several miniatures acting as a single entity.

A **squad** is a unit made up of several miniatures that share activation, actions, and objectives. Even though the miniatures may carry different weapons, they are all part of the same unit for rules purposes.

**Squad coherency and placement**

In Grand Battle mode, each miniature in a squad must stay **within 1" or less of at least one other miniature from the same squad**, forming a coherent group. As long as that chain is unbroken, the squad may adopt any formation you like: in line, in a wedge, or clustered.

![Individual miniature versus a squad]({{miniatureVsSquadImage}})

> Throughout the rulebook, whenever a rule mentions **"unit"**, it applies equally to **"squad"** in Grand Battle mode, unless stated otherwise.

### Recommended Base/Surface Sizes

ZeroLore is miniature-agnostic, but bases proportional to the unit type are recommended to maintain visual and tactical coherence.

| Unit Type | Recommended Base |
| --- | --- |
| Shock / Elite (infantry) | 25 – 40 mm round |
| Bulky Elite / Monsters | 50 – 60 mm round |
| Light Vehicles | 40 – 60 mm |
| Medium / Heavy Vehicles | 60 – 80 mm or oval |

These measurements are guidelines. The criterion is that the base is consistent with the visual size of the miniature and does not provide an unfair tactical advantage.

### Dice and Rolls

ZeroLore always uses six-sided dice (1D6) and three-sided dice for some weapons (1D3).

Throughout the game, different types of rolls are made: **Initiative**, **Precision**, **Save**, etc. Each is explained in detail in its corresponding section.

### Rule Priority

In ZeroLore there are two kinds of rules: the **general rules**, which are those in this base rulebook and apply to every unit, and the rules the units themselves have: unit specialty (the abilities written on a unit's card), weapon abilities, and faction abilities.

Whenever a rule contradicts or modifies a rule in the rulebook, **the rules on the unit card always take priority**.

*Example: the rulebook says a locked unit cannot shoot. If a weapon has the Multi-use ability, that special rule allows it to shoot even while locked — and that takes precedence.*

### Number of Players

ZeroLore can be played with **2 to 4 players**. The available formats are:

- **1vs1** — a duel between two players.
- **1vs2** — a matchup against one player.
- **2vs2** — two teams of two players.
- **1vs1vs1** — three-way free-for-all.
- **1vs1vs1vs1** — four-way free-for-all.

In games with more than two players, activation order is determined by initiative at the start of each turn, and also applies to teams.

### Game Modes

ZeroLore can be played in two main modes: **Skirmish** and **Grand Battle**.

The game mode is chosen **before the game begins**, and all players must use the same mode.

- **Skirmish mode:** each miniature acts as an independent unit. It is a more tactical and narrative mode, ideal for quick games and small tables. **In Skirmish you cannot include Vehicles, Monsters, or Artillery** — these unit types are reserved for Grand Battle mode.
- **Grand Battle mode:** units are grouped into squads that act as a single entity. This is the mode designed for larger-scale battles, where every unit type is available, including Vehicles, Monsters, and Artillery.

Unless stated otherwise, all rules in the rulebook apply to both modes.

# Measuring Distances

All distances are measured in inches (").

- Measurements are taken **from the closest point of the base** of the acting unit to the closest point of the target.
- Distances may be measured at any time during the game.
- If in doubt, either player may request a **precise re-measurement**, and the result applies definitively.

![Base-to-base measurement with a ruler]({{measurementImage}})

### Line of Sight

For a unit to make a ranged attack against another, it must have **line of sight**.

- A unit is considered to have line of sight if **any part of the target miniature can be seen** from the attacker's point of view.
- Line of sight is measured from **any point of the attacking miniature's body** (not from the weapon or the base). If any part of the target miniature can be seen from any point of the attacker's body, line of sight exists.
- Units have **360-degree** vision; the miniature's orientation does not limit its field of view.
- If a miniature (allied or enemy) or a piece of terrain completely blocks vision, the target **cannot be attacked** at range.

Cover rules are detailed later in the rulebook.

![Example of clear and blocked line of sight]({{lineOfSightImage}})

### The Golden Rule

ZeroLore is designed to be flexible. If a situation not covered by the rules comes up during a game:

- Players should resolve it using **common sense** and mutual agreement.
- If there is no consensus, a 1D6 roll can be made to decide quickly and continue the game.

# Turn Structure

A game of ZeroLore is divided into **turns**.

Each turn always follows the same structure and is resolved in an orderly way to guarantee a clear and balanced flow.

Each turn consists of the following phases:

1. **Initiative Phase**
2. **Activation Phase**
3. **End of Turn**

![Diagram of the turn flow with initiative, alternating activations, and restart]({{turnStructureImage}})

## 1. Initiative Phase

At the beginning of each turn, both players make the **Initiative roll**.

- Each player rolls **1D6**. The player or team with the highest result **acts first** during that turn. On a tie, re-roll.
- Starting with the player with the highest Initiative, each player may deploy units from Reserve at the command posts they control (free).

In team games, players on the same side may take a brief moment to coordinate their strategy before continuing.

## 2. Activation Phase

After the Initiative Phase, the **Activation Phase** begins.

- The player who won Initiative activates **first**.
- Players alternate activations, **one unit at a time**.

Activating a unit means:

- Declaring it as the active unit.
- Performing up to **two actions**, respecting the cost of each one.
- Fully resolving their effects.

Once a unit has been activated, it **cannot activate again** during that turn. To keep track of activations, each unit is marked with the **activation token** (orange side = activated, grey side = not activated). At the start of each turn, all tokens are flipped to the grey side.

![Unit with the grey and orange activation token]({{activationImage}})

## 3. End of Turn

When **all units from both players** have been activated, the turn ends.

During the End of Turn, effects that state they resolve at the end of the turn finish, and points earned are counted according to the game mode. **Scoring, victory conditions and game setup** are detailed in the **Missions** document. After this, a new turn begins, starting again with the Initiative Phase.

# Unit Actions

Each time a unit is activated during the Activation Phase, it has **2 actions**. Actions are resolved one at a time: each action must be completed before the next begins, and the same action cannot be repeated during the same activation. The unit may forgo one or both actions.

The available actions are:

| Action | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Move | 1 |
| Shoot | 1 |
| Run | 2 |
| Charge | 2 |
| Melee Attack | 2 |
| Disengage | 1 |

An action costing **2 actions** consumes the unit's entire activation. Move and Shoot are independent actions costing 1: a unit can, for example, Move and then Shoot in the same activation **without penalty**, unless an ability states otherwise.

# Movement Rules

The following rules apply whenever an action or rule allows a unit to move.

## Move

When a unit moves, it may travel up to the distance indicated by the action or rule used.

- Units may move in any direction.
- A unit cannot be moved within **1" of an enemy unit**, except when Charging.
- Movement may be used to go around obstacles or position freely on the battlefield.

## Run

The unit spends **2 actions** and may move up to its **Movement + Speed**. During this activation it cannot make attacks or end its movement within 1" or less of an enemy unit.

### Terrain and Obstacles

A unit may:

- Move around obstacles freely.
- Climb elevated surfaces if its movement allows it to physically reach the desired height.

To climb:

1. The unit must move until its base touches the base of the obstacle. Then, the vertical height it wants to climb is measured, consuming movement.
2. Units with the **Flight** ability may climb obstacles diagonally.

**Vehicles and Monsters at height.** **Vehicles cannot climb** onto structures, platforms, or elevated floors: they move only at ground level. **Monsters can climb**, as long as they **physically fit in the space** they access. If the miniature does not fit on that floor or platform — for example, its head hits the upper floor of a multi-storey building — it cannot be placed there.

# Ranged Combat

Units may make **ranged attacks** against valid targets using the weapons in their arsenal.

## Shoot

The unit spends **1 action** and makes a ranged attack. It may shoot whether or not the unit moved beforehand; moving and shooting are independent actions and do not penalise each other, unless an ability states otherwise.

A locked unit cannot take this action, unless a rule allows it, such as **Multi-use** or the Vehicle and Monster rules.

To attack at range, the unit must:

- Have **line of sight** to the target.
- Be within the **range** of the weapon used.
- Not be restricted by a special rule.

## Ranged Attack Sequence

Every ranged attack is always resolved following this sequence:

1. Choose target.
2. Determine the number of attack dice for the weapon.
3. Roll and meet or beat the precision.
4. The defender rolls saves.
5. Apply damage.

![Numbered diagram of the shooting sequence]({{rangedAttackSequenceImage}})

## 1. Choose Target

The attacking unit chooses a visible enemy unit within the weapon's range.

- A unit may attack only **one target**, unless a rule states otherwise.
- If at least one miniature in the attacking unit has line of sight, the unit may make the attack.

## 2. Determine Attacks

Each weapon indicates how many **Attacks** it makes.

- Roll as many dice as the weapon's Attacks value indicates.
- Each die represents an independent shot or projectile.

## 3. Roll Precision

For each die rolled:

- If the result is **equal to or higher than the weapon's Precision value**, the attack hits.
- A result of **6** is considered a **critical hit**, unless a rule states otherwise.
- Results below the Precision value are considered misses and are discarded.

Dice that hit are **not re-rolled**.

## 4. Roll Saves

The defending player rolls as many dice as hits the unit received.

- Each result **equal to or higher than the Save value** blocks a hit.
- Critical hits are blocked using the normal Save, unless a rule states otherwise.
- Unblocked hits go on to inflict damage.

## 5. Apply Damage

Each unblocked hit inflicts the damage indicated by the weapon.

- Normal hits inflict **base damage**.
- Critical hits inflict the weapon's **critical damage**.

When a unit loses all its Wounds, it is removed from play as destroyed.

# Melee Combat

Melee combat happens when two units are **in base contact**. At that moment, both units become **locked in combat**.

## Charge

The unit spends **2 actions** and may move up to its **Movement + Speed** toward an enemy unit. If it reaches base contact, it immediately makes a free melee attack.

If it does not reach the target, it ends its movement wherever it got to and its activation ends.

**Clarification:** any movement that ends in base contact with an enemy unit is a **charge**. It is not possible to reach contact via the Move action or by Running.

A unit may Charge a unit already locked in combat. After the charge, all units involved are considered locked in the same combat.

*Example: your Crusaders (Movement 5", Speed +2") have an enemy unit 6" away. They declare a Charge, move 7", and reach contact: they attack for free. The defender will respond during its own activation.*

![Unit making a charge]({{sprintImage}})

## Melee Attack

A locked unit spends **2 actions** to attack with one of its melee weapons.

If it is locked with several enemy units, it chooses only one as the target, unless a rule states otherwise.

## Locked Units

When two units are in base contact, they are considered locked in melee combat.

While it remains locked, a unit may only use the **Melee Attack** or **Disengage** actions, unless a rule states otherwise. If it manages to Disengage, it is no longer locked and its remaining action follows the normal rules.

Units stay locked until one is eliminated or manages to Disengage.

![Two units locked in base contact]({{lockedUnitsImage}})

## Disengage

A locked unit spends **1 action** to try to leave combat. Roll 1D6:

- On a result of **4+**, it is no longer locked. The unit may use its **remaining action** normally (Move or Shoot). If it moves, it must end more than 1" away from all enemy units.
- On a result of **1, 2, or 3**, it fails to break free: it **loses all its actions** and its activation ends immediately.

If it is locked with several enemy units, a single roll allows it to separate from all of them. If, when moving, there is no valid position more than 1" from every enemy, it will not be able to move away, but it is still disengaged.

## Resolving Melee Combat

Melee combat is resolved following this sequence:

1. The attacking unit rolls the **Attacks** dice from its melee weapon profile.
2. Results of **1 and 2** are considered misses. A result of **6** is a critical hit.
3. The defender rolls as many **Save** dice as hits it received. Critical hits are blocked using the normal Save, unless a rule states otherwise.
4. Unblocked hits inflict the attacking weapon's base or critical damage.

If the defender survives, it may attack during its own activation using its action.

## Vehicles and Monsters in Melee Combat

Vehicles and Monsters are not restricted by being locked the way other units are. Even while locked in melee combat, they may use their actions freely: **Shoot** (if they have a ranged weapon), **Melee Attack**, or any combination their actions allow. The player decides how to spend each one.

In addition, these units **can be attacked at range** even while locked in melee combat.

![Vehicle locked in melee combat that can shoot and be attacked at range]({{vehicleMonsterMeleeImage}})

## Shooting at Locked Units

A unit locked in melee combat **cannot be attacked at range** by units outside that combat, unless an ability states otherwise.

**Exception:** Vehicles and Monsters can be attacked at range even while locked in melee combat.

# Squad Rules (Grand Battle mode)

In Skirmish mode, each miniature is an independent unit and the general rules of the rulebook apply without modification. The following rules **apply only in Grand Battle mode**, when units are grouped into squads of several miniatures.

## Line of Sight in Squads

If at least one miniature of an enemy squad is visible to the attacker, the entire squad may be chosen as the target of a ranged attack.

## Using Weapons in Squads

In Grand Battle, each miniature uses the weapons it has equipped.

When a Hero is part of a squad, it resolves its attacks independently from the rest of the unit.

All miniatures in the squad attack the same target with the chosen weapon, unless a rule states otherwise.

## Melee Combat in Squads

**Choosing a target in melee combat**

In Grand Battle, even though a squad may be in contact with several enemy squads, when making a melee attack it must **choose a single target squad**.

As soon as one miniature of the squad reaches base contact with an enemy unit, **the entire squad is considered locked** and all its miniatures take part in the combat. As long as physically just one miniature of the squad is in base contact with the enemy, the whole squad takes part in the combat and makes its attacks together.

![Squad melee combat with one miniature in contact and the whole squad participating]({{squadMeleeImage}})

The attack action is considered **consumed by the entire squad**.

## Eliminating Units in a Squad

If a squad suffers damage, the player who owns the squad decides which miniature receives the damage inflicted. All the damage is applied to that miniature until it is eliminated. It is not possible to spread the damage from a single attack across several miniatures while the chosen miniature is still standing.

If the chosen miniature is eliminated and there is still damage to assign, the player must choose another miniature from the squad to apply the remaining damage to, following the same rules.

# Cover

Cover modifies the defence of the units being attacked. To benefit from cover, **the unit must have its base in direct contact with the piece of terrain acting as cover** — if there is no base contact, there is no cover.

**What counts as cover?**

A piece of terrain provides cover if its height is at least half that of the miniature seeking protection.

**Cover**

The unit improves its **Save** by 1 against ranged attacks (e.g. from 4+ to 3+), improving its chances of blocking hits.

If a unit in cover is attacked in melee, the attacker misses on results of 1, 2, or 3.

**Moving through cover**

A unit may move through a piece of terrain if it is physically taller than it. Gaps and windows may be crossed freely, as long as they are a reasonable size for the miniature to pass through — this is resolved by common sense between the players.

**Field of view**

Cover does not change the line of sight rules: if the attacker can see **any part** of the target miniature, it can be attacked, still benefiting from cover. If no part of the miniature can be seen, it cannot be attacked at range, unless a rule states otherwise.

**Clarifications**

**Vehicles and Monsters** do not benefit from cover. Their dimensions prevent any obstacle from providing effective protection, unless the unit is completely out of line of sight.

Allied or enemy miniatures that completely block vision act as obstacles for line of sight purposes. If a unit is partially visible but is not in contact with any cover, no defensive bonuses apply.

If the attacker has **clean line of sight to the whole unit** — with no part of the terrain piece standing between attacker and target — **there is no cover**, even if the unit is in physical contact with that piece. Cover requires the obstacle to block part of the shot, not merely to be nearby.

# Command Posts and Deployment

Command posts are strategic positions spread across the battlefield. They are represented on the table with the **command post token** (circle or square). As well as being objectives to conquer, they are the only points from which units can deploy. When a player conquers a command post, they place the **flag token** of their colour on top of the command post to indicate control. If the opponent recaptures it, they replace the flag with their own.

![Unit capturing a command post with its control flag]({{commandPostControlImage}})

## Headquarters

It works as a normal command post for control and deployment purposes; there must always be one mandatory Headquarters per player.

**Control at end of turn:**

At the end of each turn, if there are units from both players at the same command post, control goes to the player whose units add up to the highest total value at that post.

**Units locked in melee:** Units from both sides that are in melee combat inside a command post **do not count toward the control calculation**. They are treated as if they did not exist for the purposes of the post until the combat is resolved. On a tie, the post stays under the control of whoever held it.

To deploy a unit at a command post, the post must be under the player's control, and the unit must be in contact with it. If the post is occupied by enemy units, even if it is still owned by value, you cannot deploy there. Units that cannot deploy on the initial turn stay in **reserve**. Some units have special deployment abilities indicated on their card.

## Reserve

Units in reserve have not yet entered the battlefield. During the **Initiative Phase** of each turn, each player may deploy units from Reserve.

Units deploy in contact with a command post you control. If the post is occupied by enemy units, you cannot deploy there even if it is yours.

## Initial Deployment

At the start of the game, each player freely deploys their units from Reserve at the command posts they control.

Each player rolls **1D6** — the highest result deploys first. Players alternate deploying unit by unit until both decide to stop deploying.

Units not deployed at this point remain in **Reserve** and may be deployed on later turns for free.

# Unit Types

Every unit in ZeroLore belongs to a **type**. The unit type defines its **role on the battlefield**, as well as what it can and cannot do during the game. All units have, at minimum, **one melee attack** in their arsenal.

| Type | Description | Rules | Strong against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shock** | Basic units that go to the front line as cannon fodder. | Can conquer posts and objectives. | Specialist |
| **Elite** | Heavy infantry, ready to endure and deal heavy damage. | Can conquer posts and objectives. | Shock, Raider |
| **Specialists** | Infantry equipped with specialised weaponry to hunt specific targets. | Can conquer posts and objectives. | Elite, Vehicles |
| **Commando** | Elite assassins tasked with eliminating heroes and high-value targets. | Can conquer posts and objectives. | Hero, Monster |
| **Raider** | Units that quickly plant themselves anywhere on the map. | Can bypass obstacles or climb them diagonally. Can conquer posts and objectives. | Artillery |
| **Monsters** | Creatures of enormous power and resilience, hard to stop. | Cannot conquer or take objectives. Can shoot and be shot at while locked in melee. Does not play in Skirmish. | Commando, Raider |
| **Vehicles** | Armoured war machines, powerful but unable to take positions. | Cannot conquer or take objectives. Can shoot and be shot at while locked in melee. Does not play in Skirmish. | Shock, Elite, Commando, Raider |
| **Artillery** | Immobile siege pieces that punish from long range. | Cannot conquer or take objectives. Cannot move. Does not play in Skirmish. | Shock, Elite, Specialists, Commando, Monsters, Vehicles, Heroes, Raider |
| **Heroes** | Unique figures that lead the army and grant it their faction ability. | Have special abilities that affect the army. | — (no class advantage) |

### Special Hero Rules

- **The Hero is mandatory in a game.** Every army must include exactly one Hero.
- **Faction ability.** Each Hero carries an ability that affects all allied units while it is on the battlefield. This ability is indicated on its card. **If the Hero is eliminated, the faction ability is lost** for the rest of the game.
- In Grand Battle mode, the **Hero must mandatorily join a squad before deployment**.
- While part of a squad, its Movement, Save, and Speed values replace those of the squad. The Hero keeps its own Wounds independently — damage is always assigned first to the rest of the squad's miniatures. The Hero is the last to fall: it can only take damage when no other miniature remains in the squad.

### Class Advantage

**Advantage benefit:** when a unit attacks a class it has advantage over and the attack inflicts damage, add **+1 to the final total damage** of the attack.

It applies the same in Skirmish and in Grand Battle.

# Unit Profiles

Every unit in ZeroLore has a **profile** that defines its capabilities on the battlefield.

This profile is used whenever the unit activates, attacks, or interacts with the scenario.

A unit's profile contains the following values:

![Visual example of a unit profile with class, set, value, and statistics]({{unitProfileImage}})

### Class

Defines the unit's **functional role** within the army.

It determines whether it can capture objectives and which special rules it can apply according to its type (Shock, Elite, Specialist, Commando, Raider, Monster, Vehicle, Artillery, Hero).

### Movement

Indicates the **maximum distance**, in inches ("), the unit may move when taking the **Move** action or during the movement granted by **Disengage**.

This value is also used as the basis for:

- **Run / Charge:** Movement + Speed.

### Wounds

Represents the total amount of damage the unit can suffer before being destroyed.

- Each point of damage reduces the unit's Wounds.
- When Wounds reach **0**, the unit is removed from play as destroyed.
- Damage taken can be tracked using the **damage tokens** (-1, -3, -5, -10), placing them next to the miniature to indicate the Wounds lost.

### Save

Indicates the minimum result needed on **1D6** to block an incoming hit.

Example: a Save of **4+** blocks hits with results of **4, 5, or 6**.

### Speed

Represents the **additional distance**, in inches ("), the unit may cover when **Running** or **Charging**.

A unit with higher Speed can cover more ground in a single burst and reach more distant targets when charging.

### Squads

Indicates the **minimum and maximum number of miniatures** the unit may include when played in Grand Battle mode. This value is not used in Skirmish mode.

### Value

Represents the unit's **cost in points**.

- It is used to balance matchups.
- It determines the size and composition of armies.
- It is also used to calculate scoring when several units are at the same command post.

### Weapon Equipment

Each unit carries exactly **2 weapons** in its profile: **one ranged and one melee**, defined by the chosen set. When making an attack, the player uses the weapon matching the type of attack. It is not possible to use both weapons in the same attack.

### Weapon Sets

Each unit has three **weapon sets** that define its combat style by distributing power between its two weapons. The set does not change the unit's base statistics or its role, only its armament. Each unit's card indicates which weapons make up each of its sets. When building the list, one of these three sets is chosen for each unit:

- **Assault:** the **melee weapon is the powerful one**; the ranged weapon is minor support. For units that seek close combat.
- **Balanced:** both weapons are **evenly matched** at a medium level. Flexible, with no power spikes.
- **Ranged:** the **ranged weapon is the powerful one**; the melee weapon is a minimal defence. For units built around ranged fire.

The chosen set is kept for the whole game. As an optional variant, players may agree that the entire game is played with a **single set** for all units in both armies.

## Ranged Weapon Profiles

Ranged weapons are described by their own profile. Each weapon indicates how its attack is resolved when used. A ranged weapon profile includes:

### Weapon Name

Identifies the weapon used.

### Attacks

Number of dice rolled when making a ranged attack with this weapon. Each die represents an independent projectile or shot.

### Range

The weapon's maximum range in inches ("). The unit may only attack targets within this range.

### Precision

The minimum result needed on each die to hit.

- A result of **6** always counts as a critical hit, unless a rule states otherwise.

### Damage / Critical Damage

Indicates how many **Wounds** are lost per hit:

- **Damage**: normal hit.
- **Critical damage**: hit obtained with a result of 6 or according to the weapon's ability.

### Weapon Abilities

Special rules associated only with that weapon.

- They apply only while the weapon is operational.
- If the weapon is destroyed or silenced, these abilities cannot be used.

## Melee Weapon Profiles

Melee weapons follow a similar structure.

### Weapon Name

Identifies the weapon used.

### Attacks

Number of dice the unit rolls when attacking in melee combat.

### Damage / Critical Damage

They work the same as for ranged weapons.

### Abilities

Special rules that only affect this type of attack or this specific weapon.

## Modifiers

In ZeroLore you will see values expressed as 4+, 5+, 3+, etc. This means the minimum result needed on 1D6 for something to happen.

- **4+** → you need to roll 4, 5, or 6 → 3 out of every 6 dice → 50%
- **5+** → you need to roll 5 or 6 → 2 out of every 6 → ~33%
- **3+** → you need to roll 3, 4, 5, or 6 → 4 out of every 6 → ~67%

When a rule says **+1 to the Precision value**, the number needed goes up — it is worse for the attacker. If your weapon hits on 4+ and you get +1, you now need 5+. Conversely, **-1 to the Precision value** is an advantage: if you hit on 4+ and get -1, you now hit on 3+. This applies to Saves in the same way.

To avoid situations of immortality or infallibility, modifiers to Save and Hits have the following limits:

- **Maximum Save:** no unit can end up needing more than 6+ to save, regardless of accumulated modifiers. A result of 1 always fails the save.
- **Maximum Precision:** no unit can end up needing more than 6+ to hit, regardless of accumulated modifiers. A result of 6 always hits.

## Weapon Abilities and Unit Specialties

- A unit specialty applies whenever the unit is in play.
- Weapon abilities apply only when that weapon is used.

Weapon abilities, unit specialties, and their timing are detailed on each unit's card for easy reference during the game.

# Weapon Abilities

Some weapons have an ability that modifies how they resolve their attacks.

These abilities serve to differentiate weapons without adding complex rules. Some weapons are simple and reliable, others chase critical hits, others punch through defences better, and others are powerful but risky.

When a unit makes an attack, it must choose a single valid weapon from its profile. If that weapon has an ability, apply its effect during that attack.

| Weapon Ability | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Reliable | This weapon has no special rules. |
| Dead Angle | This weapon cannot shoot below half its range. |
| Brutal X+ | This weapon's hits count as critical on a natural result of **X+** on the attack roll. |
| Piercing | Each critical hit from this weapon worsens by **1** the Save made against that hit. This effect is not cumulative. |
| Unstable | After resolving the attack, roll 1D6. On a result of 1 or 2, the unit carrying this weapon suffers the same damage it inflicted on the target. If the attack caused no damage, there is no backlash. |
| Multi-use | This weapon may be used to take the Shoot action even while the unit is locked in melee combat. |
| Direct | This weapon hits directly and does not use Precision; therefore, it is not affected by modifiers to Precision. |
| Explosive (X) | If the target suffers damage, the attacker chooses up to **X additional miniatures**, allied or enemy, within 3" of the miniature hit; each suffers the same damage directly. In a squad, its owner assigns it following the squad's normal rules. |
| Arcing | This weapon may attack targets without direct line of sight, as long as they are within its **Range**. |
| Reach | This melee weapon may be used against any enemy unit within 3" or less, whether or not it is locked with this unit. |
| Erratic | Before resolving the attack, roll 1D6: the result is the ranged weapon's Precision during that attack. |
| Sweep | Melee weapon. When attacking, instead of choosing a target, it **attacks ALL enemy units locked with it**. Resolve the attack separately against each one. |
| Anti-X | This weapon inflicts **1D3** extra damage against the specific type indicated, as long as the weapon deals at least 1 damage to the target. |

**Note:** Direct and Erratic are abilities exclusive to **ranged weapons**; they cannot appear on melee weapons.

# Equipment

**Items** are single-use equipment bought with **Value** when building the list and assigned to a specific unit at deployment. The full catalogue of items, with their cost, is in its own section.

- Each unit may equip **only one item**.
- An army may repeat items, with a **maximum of 3 copies of the same item**. *(Example: you can equip a grenade to three different units, but not a fourth grenade.)*
- The item belongs to **the unit, not to each miniature**: a squad uses the item only once, no matter how many miniatures it has.
- Attacks and effects generated by an item (such as the Grenade) **do not benefit from action modifiers or specialties** (Move, Shoot, Accurate, Shooter…): they do exactly what their card says.
- **Timing:** each item indicates on its card **when** it may be used ("on activation", "when declared a target", "on deployment"…). If that moment passes without declaring it, the item **cannot be applied retroactively** *(see Forgotten actions, in General Considerations)*.
- After use, the item is discarded.

# Faction Abilities

**Each Hero carries its own faction ability**, which defines the army's playstyle while the Hero is on the battlefield.

- The faction ability applies automatically to all allied units — it requires no activation and costs no actions.
- If the Hero is eliminated, the ability is lost for the rest of the game.
- Each Hero's faction ability is indicated on its unit card.

Choosing which Hero to bring is the first major strategic decision of the game.

## General Considerations

**1. Forgotten actions:** *If a player forgets to take an action, use an ability, or declare an effect at the proper time, it is considered lost and cannot be recovered, unless both players mutually agree.*

**2. Aesthetic consistency:** *Although the game is completely agnostic regarding miniatures, it is recommended that the army has visual coherence. The player is free to use whatever they want.*

**3. Communicating intentions:** *Communicating the intentions behind each play helps keep the game flowing and clear.*

**4. Timer:** *As an optional variant, a timer of 1 to 2 minutes per activation can be used to speed up decision-making.*
